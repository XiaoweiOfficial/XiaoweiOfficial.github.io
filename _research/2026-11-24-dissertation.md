collection: research
type: "Dissertation"
excerpt: "My dissertation argues that complement provision does not unfold in an undifferentiated platform space. Platform participants make decisions within *platform-categories* — local arenas of demand, learning, competition, and evaluation. These categories condition where platform owners intervene, how independent complementors respond, and how complementors adapt their portfolios over time. Three connected studies, drawn from the U.S. console video game industry between 1995 and 2005, examine when platform owners enter categories with their own complements, when complementors respond more favorably to that entry, and how complementors reposition within categories against competing benchmarks. Tilburg University; supervised by Niels Noorderhaven and Xavier Martin. Defense scheduled for 24 November 2026."
